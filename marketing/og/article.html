Why the brain needs sleep to remember

A long read · 14 min

Every night, while we are unconscious, the brain replays the day. In the hippocampus, the same groups of neurons that fired while we explored a new place fire again during slow-wave sleep, compressed in time, as if the day were being rehearsed at high speed.

That replay is not idle. Each burst is timed against sleep spindles and slow oscillations in the cortex, and the coupling between them appears to be what moves a memory from short-term storage in the hippocampus into the long-term networks of the neocortex.

Researchers can now disrupt or boost this coupling, and the results are striking: weaken it and people forget more of what they learned the day before; strengthen it with gently timed sounds and recall improves.

Sleep also appears to prune. Synapses that grew stronger during waking hours are scaled back overnight, which saves energy and keeps the signal that matters from drowning in noise.
